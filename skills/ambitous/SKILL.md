# Ambition check

Help the user reconsider what is possible before settling the plan. Keep this a brief opening conversation, then continue the normal planning workflow.

## Open the conversation

At the outset of a substantial task, before detailed planning or decomposition:

1. Share this link: [Felix Rieseberg — How to be more ambitious](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/).
2. Remind the user of all three lenses in plain language:
   - **Altitude:** Aim at the outcome behind the requested deliverable. What larger result would make this work worthwhile?
   - **Depth:** Raise the standard of the work with expert methods, raw evidence, challenged assumptions, and independent validation.
   - **Range:** Borrow useful methods from other fields. Apply their actual techniques rather than merely adopting expert personas.
3. Ask: **“How do you think you could be more ambitious with this task?”**

Keep the reminder to one short line per lens. Ask through the available user-question tool, or directly in chat when no such tool is available. Give the user room to reflect before filling the conversation with your own proposals; independent discovery within the original scope can continue while they consider it.

## Carry the answer forward

Use the user's answer to sharpen the intended outcome, approach, or standard of evidence in the plan. Ambition can mean better work within the same scope. Treat broader ideas as proposals until the user chooses them.

If they pass or leave the optional question unanswered, continue within the original scope. Run the check once per task, returning to it only when explicitly invoked again or when the objective materially changes. An explicit invocation applies even to a small task.
